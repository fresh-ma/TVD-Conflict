\section{Extract Safety-Judge Failure-Modes from Attack Responses}

We evaluate responses from ten jailbreak attacks: Cognitive Overload,
MathPrompt, Semantic Cloaking, ActorBreaker, Mastermind, Compound Roleplay, CIA
Context, AutoDAN-Turbo, FITD, and Prefill. Each attack contributes 200 responses,
covering four victim models and 50 AdvBench goals. Every response is evaluated
by GPT-OSS-120B, Qwen3.7-Flash, and DeepSeek-V4-Flash using both the binary and
graded judge prompts.

Panel A of Table~\ref{tab:attack-judge-asr-conflict} reports the ASR assigned by
each backbone under the two rubrics. The choice of rubric can reverse the
apparent ranking of attack methods. For GPT-OSS-120B, for example, MathPrompt
falls from 67.5\% under binary judging to 29.5\% under scoring, while Mastermind
rises from 31.5\% to 87.5\%. Panel B isolates evaluator conflict from ASR: it
holds the rubric fixed and measures disagreement across the three backbones for
each attack.

We derive the failure-mode taxonomy from conflict cases rather than from all
attack responses. Each case retains the shared query and response, the raw judge
outputs, parsed verdicts, and the dimensions responsible for disagreement. This
representation supports both quantitative prevalence estimates and qualitative
inspection of how an evaluator reached its decision.
