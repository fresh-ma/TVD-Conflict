\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Automated Jailbreak Evaluation.}
Automated evaluation is now a central component of jailbreak research. Attack
frameworks such as PAIR and TAP use LLM judge feedback to guide their search
process \citep{chao2023jailbreaking,mehrotra2023tree}. HarmBench and
JailbreakBench standardize evaluation across harmful goals, attack methods, and
target models through prompted judges or dedicated classifiers
\citep{mazeika2024harmbench,chao2024jailbreakbench}. Subsequent work has refined
the definition and measurement of attack success through structured scoring
criteria \citep{souly2024strongreject,cai2024rethinking} and consensus among
multiple evaluators \citep{lin2025efficient}. These methods enable red teaming
at scale, but they also place the validity of reported attack success
rates on the reliability of the judge.

\paragraph{Reliability of LLM Judges.}
General studies of LLM evaluators have documented position and verbosity biases,
as well as a tendency to prefer their own outputs \citep{zheng2023judging}.
They are also vulnerable to adversarial input perturbations
\citep{raina2024is} and exhibit broad sensitivity across controlled bias
dimensions \citep{ICLR2025_fdca08d3}. These problems become especially
consequential in jailbreak evaluation. Safety evaluation pipelines exhibit
substantial methodological noise \citep{beyer2025llm}, while red teaming
distribution shifts can reduce judge accuracy to nearly random chance
\citep{schwinn2026coin}. Benign wrappers and refusal statements can also reverse
verdicts without changing the underlying harmful content
\citep{gao2026reliable}. Other studies identify evaluator failures caused by
semantic reframing, degraded responses, and rigid safety priors
\citep{souly2024strongreject,cai2024rethinking,alloula2026safety,almasoud2026security}.
Together, these works establish that jailbreak judges are unreliable, but they
primarily diagnose failures on fixed datasets, human labels, or predefined
perturbations. They do not actively search the broader response space for new
content that exposes recurring failures across judges. \textsc{TVD}-Conflict
addresses this gap by making disagreement under a shared evaluation criterion
an executable search objective, enabling active discovery and analysis of judge
failure cases without human annotation.
