\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{LLM as Jailbreak Judge.}
With the rapid expansion of adversarial safety evaluation for large language models, \textsc{LLM-as-a-Judge} has become the standard infrastructure for quantifying attack success rates (ASR). In automated red teaming, black-box attack frameworks such as PAIR \citep{chao2023jailbreaking} and TAP \citep{mehrotra2023tree} rely on LLM judges to provide optimization feedback. In standardized safety benchmarking, frameworks including HarmBench \citep{mazeika2024harmbench} and JailbreakBench \citep{chao2024jailbreakbench} deploy state-of-the-art frontier models or dedicated safety classifiers to automate evaluations across diverse threat categories. Recent efforts further investigate multi-agent debate mechanisms \citep{lin2025efficient} to enhance judgment consensus. These pipelines universally operate under the assumption that judge models provide stable and faithful safety assessments across diverse response styles.

\paragraph{Vulnerabilities and Disagreements in LLM Judges.}
Despite widespread adoption, extensive research shows that model-based evaluators are susceptible to substantial systematic flaws in general domains. Prior meta-evaluations demonstrate that LLM judges exhibit inherent biases such as position bias, verbosity bias, and self-enhancement \citep{zheng2023judging}, while remaining vulnerable to minor adversarial perturbations \citep{raina2024is}. \citet{ICLR2025_fdca08d3} identify 12 distinct types of bias and introduce the CALM benchmark to quantify judge sensitivity under controlled perturbations. 
In safety and jailbreak evaluation, these vulnerabilities become even more pronounced. Recent analyses reveal that safety judges are easily deceived by superficial compliance disclaimers, semantic reframing, or degraded incoherence from victim models \citep{souly2024strongreject,cai2024rethinking}, with judge accuracy degrading to near random chance under red-teaming distribution shifts \citep{schwinn2026coin,almasoud2026security}. Furthermore, safety-judge decisions often remain anchored to rigid internal priors when the provided context or safety criteria vary, showing reluctance to revise evaluations when explicit definitions conflict with their default boundaries \citep{alloula2026safety}.
Although emerging efforts have begun benchmarking judge unreliability through static stress tests and human audits \citep{schwinn2026coin}, they fail to systematically examine what specific content triggers instability across judges, nor do they provide an automated mechanism to actively mine and categorize these boundary failures. To bridge this gap, TVD-Conflict introduces an agentic framework that leverages inter-judge disagreement as an automated validator to dynamically discover conflict-inducing pairs and systematically unveil their underlying failure modes.
