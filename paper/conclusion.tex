\section{Conclusion}

Safety evaluation with LLM judges is not invariant to the evaluator. Across ten
jailbreak attacks, three judge backbones and two rubrics produce materially
different decisions on identical interactions. Cross-judge Conflict Rate is
24.2\% under binary judging and 10.8\% under scoring, with substantially higher
rates for particular attacks. The variation is strongly attack dependent,
indicating that a single aggregate ASR can conceal systematic blind spots.
TVD-Conflict turns these discrepancies into a data-generation objective and
supports analysis through six operational failure modes. The resulting evidence
argues for evaluating safety judges as fallible measurement instruments rather
than treating their outputs as interchangeable ground truth.
