\section{Conclusion}

In this work, we study disagreement among LLM-based jailbreak judges and
introduce \textsc{TVD}-Conflict, an agentic framework that uses disagreement as
automatic feedback to discover evaluator-sensitive query--response pairs. Our
experiments across ten attacks, three judge backbones, and two evaluation
protocols show that judge conflicts are frequent, structured, and present even
across independent evaluations by the same backbone. \textsc{TVD}-Conflict
collects 1,702 conflict pairs without reproducing an attack and victim-model
pipeline for every candidate, enabling us to identify four primary failure
modes and two cross-cutting defects involving criterion interpretation,
misleading response cues, contextual or representational complexity, and
incoherent judge reasoning. These findings establish judge disagreement as a
practical signal for constructing diagnostic stress tests and improving the
reliability of jailbreak evaluation.
